**Ceiling A --- success rate.** If a perfect chooser picked the right mode for every
task, success would run 5.9--51.9\% against a best single mode of 2.2--35.6\%; the
headroom is +3.45 to +16.35pp. That column is a **six-arm union quoted against a
one-arm baseline**, and the arm-matched comparison from the previous section sits
in the same table row precisely so the two cannot be read apart.

**Ceiling B --- cost at matched success.** Keep the best mode everywhere and send
only the tasks *no* mode solves to the cheapest one. Success is unchanged **by
construction**, and cost falls 9.5--30.6\% in **8 of 8 cells**. No arm is added, so
the arm-count objection does not reach this ceiling.

**Why both are hard to reach.** No mode solves 48.1--94.1\% of tasks. The subset
where a per-task choice even exists --- more than one mode solves it --- is
1.5--34.6\% of a cell, and on two cells that is 3 and 4 tasks.
