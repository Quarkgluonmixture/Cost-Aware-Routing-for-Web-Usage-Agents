\caption{Cascade signal against a random-escalation control. Is the cascade's signal doing anything? Table~\ref{tab:t20} answers a deployment question: no operating point beats always-rich on success rate: which on its own cannot distinguish a signal that carries nothing from one that carries something insufficient. This is the comparator that separates them: the same escalation budget spent at random. \textbf{Caution:} Read the median column, not the best column. The best margin is positive in 24 of 24 (cell × fraction) combinations, but each of those is a maximum over that cell's 8--10 candidate signals against a constant (the random comparator does not depend on which signal is used) so positivity there is partly arithmetic. Over all 222 (signal × cell × fraction) points, 64.4\% are positive with a median of +0.327pp: that is the unselected statement, and it is the one to quote. On cls·B2, red·B0, WA·B1 the median is negative at one or more fractions while the best is positive: there the apparent win is entirely selection. The right-hand column is how much of the gap to a per-task oracle the best signal recovers, and inherits the same caveat. The offline-splice caveat on Table~\ref{tab:t20} applies unchanged. Source: \texttt{confidence\_cascade\_with\_wa.json}.}
