\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{How web agents observe the page.} Agents have read HTML or accessibility trees \citep{deng2023mind2web,agentoccam2025}, screenshots grounded to elements \citep{zheng2024seeact,he2024webvoyager,zheng2024uground}, or set-of-marks overlays that fuse the two \citep{yang2023som,koh2024visualwebarena}. A parallel line shrinks or reshapes the text observation \citep{kerboua2025focusagent,schiepanski2025d2snap,enomoto2026observation}, and some systems combine several grounding routes \citep{li2026avenirweb}. OSWorld compares screenshot, accessibility-tree, combined and set-of-marks inputs across models and finds the best input model-dependent \citep{xie2024osworld}, and \citet{chi2024elementordering} compare screenshot-plus-text with text only on a VisualWebArena subset. Closer to adaptivity, \citet{li2026aoi} gate visual observation components during execution of computer-use tasks, and \citet{liu2026adaptivevlm} route each action of a computer-use agent among vision-language models; neither chooses between text and screenshot per task before it runs. These works compare observation designs at the level of a benchmark score or adapt them online. Our contribution is not that the best observation varies, which OSWorld already shows, but a matched removal of the screenshot, repeated task-level outcomes, a test of task structure beyond difficulty, and a deployable per-task choice judged against mixtures of the fixed choices.

\paragraph{Routing and cascades.} Choosing among models per query to trade quality for cost is well studied for language models \citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2025routellm,ding2024hybridllm,gupta2024cascades,peale2026flexibleRouting}; see \citet{moslem2026routingsurvey} for a survey. For web agents, \citet{webrouter2025} learn query-specific routing for cost-sensitive agents, and \citet{hajimiri2026budgetmatched} judge agent components at a matched budget. Early exit \citep{lu2025earlyexit} stops agents that are unlikely to succeed --- the difficulty lever that, in our data, is the only part of routing that leaves room above the fixed frontier. Routing benchmarks compare routers against random mixtures of fixed models \citep{hu2024routerbench,jitkrittum2025uniroute}, and under unified conditions several learned routers fail to beat a simple baseline \citep{li2026llmrouterbench}; \citet{lee2026routinggap} finds that assigning one model per task type in advance recovers most of the oracle routing gain on question answering, and that reruns rescore 5\% of model--question pairs. Item-response routers model query difficulty and model ability \citep{song2025irtrouter}. We route over observation representations rather than models, take the mixture baseline from this literature, and add what it rarely has: reruns of identical agent conditions and a test of per-task structure beyond difficulty.

\paragraph{Reliability of agent evaluation.} Repeated trials of the same agent disagree, which $\tau$-bench measures with pass$^k$ \citep{yao2025taubench}; inference itself is not deterministic even at fixed settings \citep{he2025nondeterminism,yuan2025numerical}, prompts change outcomes in ways unrelated to content \citep{sclar2024promptformat}, and agent benchmarks carry evaluator and task defects \citep{elhattami2025webarenaverified,bhat2026benchmarkingbenchmarks}. Our reruns quantify the first for web agents at the task level and show what it does to per-task routing labels; the harness defects we found are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:harness}.
