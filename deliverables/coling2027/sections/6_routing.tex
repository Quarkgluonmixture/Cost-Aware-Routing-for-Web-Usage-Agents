\section{Deployable routers do not reliably convert the signal}\label{sec:routing}

\paragraph{The comparison a router must win.} A router that sends some tasks to an expensive mode and others to a cheap one trades success for cost. Beating every fixed mode on both axes is too strict, but beating the fixed modes one at a time is too lenient: sending a random share of tasks to each of two modes is also a deployable policy. We therefore compare against the \emph{frontier of the fixed modes and all their random mixtures}: the upper concave envelope of the fixed points in the (cost, success) plane, which is the non-decreasing convex hull that model-routing benchmarks use as a baseline \citep{hu2024routerbench,jitkrittum2025uniroute}. We report how far above it a router reaches (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}a; definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:estimators}).

\paragraph{Routers.} Two constructions use 18 features available before a task runs: 14 intent-keyword indicators, the intent length, and three statistics of the first page. \emph{Per-arm heads} fit one logistic success model per mode and send a task to the cheapest mode whose predicted success clears a threshold. \emph{Triage} predicts whether any mode can solve the task, sends predicted-hopeless tasks to the cheapest mode and the rest to the best one. All predictions are out-of-fold (task-held-out five-fold).

\paragraph{Room above the frontier exists in hindsight.} Sweeping each router's threshold traces a curve. Over the three deployment modes, the triage curve pooled over the eight cells rises 1.69 points above the frontier at low budget (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}b), which a label-shuffle null that refits the whole curve does not produce ($p=0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:routing}). The per-arm heads reach 0.86 points ($p=0.072$). Triage does not choose a representation for a task; it decides which tasks are not worth the expensive mode. That one construction passes its null and the other does not is not a test that representation fit carries nothing, since a difference in significance is not a significant difference. In these data, however, the only opportunity that clears a null is the one a difficulty-only decision exposes, and it does so under billed cost only (see Robustness).

\paragraph{A deployable router does not capture it.} A curve's best point is chosen after seeing test outcomes. When the operating point is instead chosen on training folds only, the pooled gain is +0.23 points for per-arm heads and +0.59 for triage, and neither interval excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:routing}). Over all six modes both deployable routers sit slightly below the frontier ($-0.38$ and $-0.39$). % routing_gain_upper_bounds pooled
The pattern holds for the strongest backbone: for GPT-5.6 on classifieds, where the screenshot's value was largest (\S\ref{sec:screenshot}), the deployable routers lose 1.6--2.5 points; on both shopping cells, filtered or on all tasks, they stay within 1.3 points of it.

\paragraph{Robustness.} Holding tasks of the same template out together lowers the six-mode per-arm router's mean deployable gain from $-0.38$ to $-1.60$ points, and fitting on one run and scoring on its rerun gives point estimates of both signs whose intervals all include zero (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). % routing_crossrun_template_validation
Repricing cost changes the hindsight result but not the deployable one (Table~\ref{tab:sens}). Under wall-clock cost the pooled triage curve no longer clears its null (+0.68 points, $p=0.42$), nor does it under GPU time on the five locally served cells (+1.24, $p=0.39$); the per-arm heads stay just short of significance under every basis ($p=0.072$--$0.077$). Over all cells of each basis the deployable gains stay between $-0.5$ and $+1.5$ points. Dropping the two cells of the weakest backbone, which solves too few tasks for any router to gain, leaves the billed picture unchanged (triage curve +1.85, $p=0.001$; deployable +0.50 and +0.39). % routing_three_arm{,_wallclock,_gpu_time}
One interval in Table~\ref{tab:routing} (per-arm heads, three modes pooled) has an upper end below its point estimate, a sign that the task bootstrap of a policy whose threshold is re-selected in every draw is biased; we therefore read all deployable intervals as magnitudes and make no significance claim from them, in either direction.
