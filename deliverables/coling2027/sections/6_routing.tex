\section{Deployable routers show no reliable gain}\label{sec:routing}

\paragraph{The comparison a router must win.} A router that sends some tasks to an expensive mode and others to a cheap one trades success for cost. Beating every fixed mode on both axes is too strict. Beating the fixed modes one at a time is too lenient, because sending a random share of tasks to each of two modes is also a deployable policy. We therefore compare against the \emph{frontier of the fixed modes and all their random mixtures}: the upper concave envelope of the fixed modes' points in the (cost, success) plane. Model-routing benchmarks and method papers use this non-decreasing convex hull as a baseline \citep{hu2024routerbench,jitkrittum2025uniroute}. We report how far a router rises above the frontier (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}a; definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:estimators}).

\paragraph{Routers.} Both routers use 18 features available before a task runs: 14 intent-keyword indicators, the intent length, and three statistics of the first page. \emph{Per-arm heads} fit one logistic success model per mode and send a task to the cheapest mode whose predicted success clears a threshold. \emph{Triage} predicts whether any mode can solve the task and sends predicted-hopeless tasks to the cheapest mode and the rest to the best one. All predictions are out-of-fold, over five folds of tasks. Both learn from one run's outcomes, which \S\ref{sec:preference} found to measure per-task preferences poorly.

\paragraph{Room above the frontier exists in hindsight.} Pooled over cells, only triage finds room that clears a null, and only under billed cost (see Robustness). Triage's decision is about difficulty, not representation: it decides which tasks are not worth the expensive mode. Sweeping each router's threshold traces a curve. Over the three deployment modes, the triage curve pooled over the eight cells rises 1.69 points above the frontier at low budget (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}b). This rise clears a label-shuffle null that refits the whole curve ($p=0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:routing}). The per-arm heads reach 0.86 points ($p=0.072$). That the per-arm heads miss their null does not show that representation fit carries nothing: a difference in significance is not a significant difference.

\paragraph{A deployable router does not capture it.} A curve's best point is chosen after seeing test outcomes. When the operating point (threshold) is instead chosen on training folds only, the pooled gain falls to +0.23 points for per-arm heads and +0.59 for triage, against 0.86 and 1.69 in hindsight (Table~\ref{tab:routing}). The per-arm interval has its upper end below its point estimate, a sign that the task bootstrap is biased when the threshold is re-selected in every draw. We therefore read all deployable intervals as magnitudes and make no significance claim from them, in either direction. Over all six modes both deployable routers sit slightly below the frontier ($-0.38$ and $-0.39$). % routing_gain_upper_bounds pooled
The pattern holds for the strongest backbone: for GPT-5.6 on classifieds, where the screenshot's value was largest (\S\ref{sec:screenshot}), the deployable routers lose 1.63--2.45 points. On both shopping cells, filtered or on all tasks, they stay within 1.3 points of the frontier.

\paragraph{Robustness.} Over six modes, holding out tasks of the same template together lowers the per-arm heads' mean deployable gain from $-0.38$ to $-1.60$ points. Fitting on one run and scoring on its rerun, with the same template folds, gives per-cell point estimates of both signs, which average below zero (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). % routing_crossrun_template_validation
Repricing cost changes the hindsight result but not the deployable one (Table~\ref{tab:sens}). The pooled triage curve no longer clears its null under wall-clock cost (+0.68 points, $p=0.42$) or under GPU time on the five locally served cells (+1.24, $p=0.39$). The per-arm heads stay just short of significance under every basis ($p=0.072$--$0.077$). Under every basis, the deployable gains pooled over all its cells stay between $-0.5$ and $+1.5$ points. Dropping the two cells of the weakest backbone (B2) leaves the billed-cost picture unchanged (triage curve +1.85, $p=0.001$; deployable +0.50 and +0.39). % routing_three_arm{,_wallclock,_gpu_time}
