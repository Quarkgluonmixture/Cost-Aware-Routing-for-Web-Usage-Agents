\begin{abstract}
A web agent can read a page as text, as a screenshot, or both, and deployments usually fix one choice for all tasks. We measure that choice on three sites of VisualWebArena and WebArena with four vision-language backbones, comparing against screenshot-free variants and identical reruns to see which differences survive.
% sources: routing_three_arm, visual_intent_routing, task_mode_interaction_null, label_instability, routing_gain_upper_bounds
First, no observation choice is best everywhere. The screenshot's value is real, reproducible and, on one site, predictable in advance. A zero-cost rule over the task text flags classifieds tasks where adding the screenshot alone raises success by 25--30 points for the two strongest backbones (11 for a 4B model). For the 235B backbone the gap reappears on a full rerun. Second, per-task preferences between representations are reproducible beyond an additive model of task difficulty. Yet single-run labels measure them with reliability 0.31--0.50 and, over six modes, flip most where the choice can change success, as that model predicts. Third, two lightweight routers choose a representation per task from pre-run features. With their operating point fixed on training data, they show no reliable gain over the fixed choices and their random mixtures. We describe the rerun-based protocol that keeps value, reliability and routing gain apart.
\end{abstract}
