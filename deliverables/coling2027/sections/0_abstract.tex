\begin{abstract}
A web agent can read a page as text, look at a screenshot, or do both, and deployments usually fix one choice for every task. We measure that choice across three sites of VisualWebArena and WebArena with four vision-language backbones, using screenshot-free variants as controls and rerunning identical conditions to see which differences survive.

Three findings separate questions that are usually merged. First, no observation choice is best everywhere. The screenshot's value is real, reproducible and, on one site, predictable in advance. A zero-cost rule over the task text flags classifieds tasks where adding the screenshot alone raises success by 25--30 points for the two strongest backbones (11 for a 4B model). For the 235B backbone the gap reappears on a full rerun. Second, per-task preferences between representations are reproducible beyond an additive model of task difficulty. Yet single-run labels measure them with reliability 0.31--0.50 and, over six modes, flip most where the choice can change success, as that model predicts. Third, two lightweight routers choose a representation per task from pre-run features. With their operating point fixed on training data, they show no reliable gain over the fixed choices and their random mixtures. We describe the rerun-controlled protocol that keeps these three questions apart.
\end{abstract}
