## 原文

### Meta review (AC xab8)

> The authors perform a rigorous evaluation of the different modes of observation by the agents, e.g., through text, pixels, or both, across VisualWebArena and WebArena to understand when the routing policies succeed or fail and provide a concrete evaluation of how the observation methods perform. The authors also analyze the run-to-run noise and hence show very clearly the limitations of the test setup. While Reviewer 2 observes that failure to improve the performance metric over a fixed policy does not necessarily mean the routing is unhelpful and that the authors have not fully explored the trade-offs between the success rate and the cost, I still tend to gravitate towards an accept rating since this is in keeping with the spirit of the workshop, where unfinished work with interesting experiments is welcome, and, in this case, the authors have provided a rigorous and honest evaluation of their test setup.

### Reviewer TtQ2 — "Evaluating Representation Routing for Web Agents: Strengths, Run-to-Run Noise, and Data Bottlenecks"

> This submission provides a rigorous evaluation of observation modes for web agents across VisualWebArena and WebArena, analyzing when and why routing policies succeed or fail. The paper's key strengths lie in its thorough empirical setup and honest evaluation. By testing six observation modes across eight site-model settings, the authors offer concrete evidence on how different visual and text representations perform. A particularly strong contribution is the analysis of run-to-run noise (12-14% variance), which provides a crucial reality check for ceiling claims in multi-arm evaluation setups. Furthermore, the cost-bound findings present practical value for real-world deployment by showing how to cut costs without sacrificing success rates. On the flip side, the conclusions regarding routing failure feel somewhat constrained by current model limits. The main limitation is that the negative results for routing policies stem from a lack of positive supervision labels generated by weaker base agents, meaning these bounds may not hold as web agents improve. Overall, this is a clear, well-scoped piece of work that offers valuable methodology and realistic bounds for the web agent community.

### Reviewer 1MR9 — "Useful rerun controls, but routing conclusion needs stronger evidence"

> This paper studies whether web agents benefit from choosing their observation representation per task. Its focus on grounding reliability and evaluation is relevant to VLM4RWD's deployment-oriented scope.
>
> **Pros**
> - The same-mode rerun control is a useful contribution. Gains from adding representations should be compared with gains from additional attempts.
> - The text/prompt controls and detailed failure audits help interpret differences beyond aggregate success rates.
>
> **Cons**
> - The negative results are useful, but the central explanation for routing failure remains tentative. The experiments establish limitations of the tested setup more clearly than a broader relationship between routing value and learnability.
> - The evaluation does not fully explore useful trade-offs between success rate and cost. Failing to outperform a fixed policy on both metrics does not necessarily make routing unhelpful.
> - The rerun controls have limited coverage, weakening the broader claim that run-to-run variation explains much of the apparent benefit of representation diversity.
>
> I find this measurement study useful, but the headline explanation for routing failure is not yet established.
