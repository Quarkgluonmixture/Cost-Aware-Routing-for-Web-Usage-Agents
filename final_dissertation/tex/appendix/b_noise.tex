\chapter{Noise-Floor Derivation and Scope}
\label{app:noise}

\emph{Referenced from Section~\ref{sec:noise-floor}.} The main text states the
repetition thresholds and applies them. This appendix derives them and records
the scope restrictions that govern which comparisons the floors license, because
the most common way to misuse a noise floor is to do arithmetic across rows that
do not share a scope.

\section{Derivation of the null spread}

Two runs of one condition are exchangeable under the null that nothing changed
between them. Let $d$ be the number of tasks on which they disagree and $n$ the
scored-set size. Each discordant task contributes $+1/n$ or $-1/n$ to the
success-rate difference with probability $\tfrac12$ each, so with
$X\sim\mathrm{Binom}(d,\tfrac12)$ counting the flips in one direction,
\[
  D = \frac{2X-d}{n},\qquad
  \mathbb{E}[D]=0,\qquad
  \mathrm{Var}(D)=\frac{4\,\mathrm{Var}(X)}{n^{2}}=\frac{d}{n^{2}},
\]
giving Equation~\ref{eq:floor-sd}, $\mathrm{SD}(D)=\sqrt{d}/n$. The one-sided
95\% point is $1.645\,\mathrm{SD}(D)$.

Two properties of this null are worth stating explicitly. It assumes
\emph{only} exchangeability --- no model of the agent, the site, or the failure
distribution enters. And it does \emph{not} model environment drift, which is
one-directional and which we separately observe: a restarted partial run of one
mode shows 3 flips in one direction and 0 in the other on its 26 shared tasks.
Where drift is present the true spread exceeds $\sqrt{d}/n$, so the thresholds
in Table~\ref{tab:floor-spread} are themselves lower bounds.

\section{Two floors, two questions}

The inventory carries two distinct floor quantities and they answer different
questions. Conflating them was the error that an earlier draft of our own
analysis made.

\begin{description}
\item[Set-difference floor.]
  $\mathrm{self\_drop}(a\!\to\!b)=|\{a\}\setminus\{b\}|/n$. This matches
  Equation~\ref{eq:dropone} in form, so it is the floor for \emph{ceiling}
  claims --- what adding one more arm buys. Measured range across our three
  replicated arms: 4.91--7.59\pp.
\item[Mean-difference floor.] $|\mathrm{SR}(a)-\mathrm{SR}(b)|$ on the same
  pairs. This is the floor for claims comparing \emph{mean success rates}
  between modes. Measured range: 0.89--2.23\pp{} --- but each value is
  \emph{one draw} of a quantity whose standard deviation is 2.32--2.53\pp, which
  is why the interpretable threshold is 3.82--4.15\pp{} rather than 2.23\pp.
\end{description}

Reading a 2.2\pp{} effect against a 2.23\pp{} ``measured floor'' compares a draw
to a draw. The main text uses the threshold, not the band.

\section{Scope restrictions}

\paragraph{Do not do arithmetic across rows.} Each floor carries its own scope
--- backbone, site, mode, $n$, and serving path. The only comparisons drawn
anywhere in this dissertation are within one (model, site) cell at equal arm
count.

\paragraph{The WebArena row is not a floor for its own baseline.} It pools five
mode-specific pilot-versus-full comparisons over the same 10 registered pilot
tasks ($\text{pooled }n=50$ over ten distinct tasks), while the distinct-arm
figure beside it uses 104 tasks starting from \mDOM{}. \mDOM{}'s own
pilot-versus-full comparison shows zero flips in either direction; the 2--4\pp{}
band is generated entirely by \mVis{} and \mPP{}, and the five panels are
correlated through the same ten tasks. It should be read as \emph{some arms of
this cell move by 2--4\pp{} under repetition}.

\paragraph{The five-arm gain is never set against a one-rerun floor.} We hold one
rerun arm, not five, and repetition has its own diminishing returns. The ceiling
is reported with its arm count attached (Table~\ref{tab:oracle},
Figure~\ref{fig:f8}); the like-for-like one-arm comparison is
Figure~\ref{fig:f10b}.

\section{The determinism claim we retracted}

Our project record previously held that the locally served backbones were
deterministic across reruns, on the grounds that decoding is greedy
(\texttt{do\_sample}$=$false) and step-level outputs had been verified identical
in a controlled check. That verification was correct and the inference from it
was not. An episode carries site state, wall-clock and session lifetime in
addition to model output; the WebArena pilot-versus-full overlap shows flips on
\mVis{} and \mPP{} despite greedy decoding. Step-level determinism and
episode-level determinism are different properties, and only the first was ever
evidenced.
