\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{The problem}
\label{sec:problem}

A web agent cannot act on a page it has not been shown. Before every click it
must be handed some encoding of the current screen, and there is more than one
way to produce that encoding. The page can be flattened into structured text
drawn from the browser's accessibility tree; it can be rendered as a screenshot
with candidate elements numbered and boxed; it can be sent as both. These are
not equivalent inputs. They differ in what they make visible, and they differ in
what they cost.

The choice is normally made once, at configuration time, and applied to every
step of every task. That is the problem this dissertation is about. A multi-step
web task takes tens of browser steps, and the representation decision is
re-paid at each one: the screenshot is re-encoded, the marks are re-drawn, the
tokens are re-billed. If the extra information changed the outcome at every
step, this would simply be the price of the capability. But it is not obvious
that it does, and --- as far as we can establish --- nobody has measured whether
it does.

The obvious response is to decide per step instead of once. That intuition is
what makes this an interesting problem rather than an engineering preference,
because it is only correct if three separate conditions hold at the same time.
The cheap and expensive encodings must genuinely succeed on \emph{different}
tasks, or there is nothing to select between. The need for the expensive one
must be \emph{predictable} from something observable before it is paid for, or
the selection cannot be made in time. And the value recovered must exceed what
the selection machinery itself costs, or the whole apparatus loses money. Each
condition is measurable. This dissertation measures all three, in that order,
and reports where the chain breaks.

\section{Why the cost is worth arguing about}
\label{sec:stakes}

Three properties of the setting turn a per-step configuration choice into a
system-level quantity.

\paragraph{The cost is multiplicative in the episode length.} Our agent
averages 13.7--28.4 steps per task depending on site, model and mode
(Chapter~\ref{ch:system}, Table~\ref{tab:cost-per-mode}). A decision that costs
a fraction of a cent per step is a decision made hundreds of thousands of times
across a benchmark sweep, and millions of times in any deployment worth the
name.

\paragraph{The expensive part is concentrated, not diffuse.} It is tempting to
assume the annotated-screenshot condition is expensive because it sends more of
everything. It does not. Measured on matched pages, the text payload of
the full Set-of-Marks mode is a median $1.008\times$ that of the structured-text
mode on classifieds and $1.004\times$ on reddit (Section~\ref{sec:obs-space};
$n=429$ paired tasks). The cost difference between them is therefore almost
entirely the image. That is a useful fact rather than a curiosity: it means
the expensive component can be removed \emph{surgically}, holding the text and
the prompt fixed, which is exactly the manipulation Chapter~\ref{ch:system}
builds.

\paragraph{Computational cost is not the same thing as environmental cost, and
this dissertation does not conflate them.} We report token counts, billed
dollars, wall-clock latency, and --- for the locally served backbones only ---
device-level energy drawn from GPU telemetry. We do not convert tokens into
kilowatt-hours; no hardware-independent conversion exists
\citep{fernandez2025energy}, and treating one as a proxy for the other is a
known failure mode in this literature \citep{lannelongue2021green,
henderson2020systematic}. Section~\ref{sec:sustainability} states exactly what
our measurement boundary includes and what it therefore cannot support.

\section{Concepts before machinery}
\label{sec:concepts}

Three ideas carry the whole argument, and each can be stated before any
implementation detail.

\paragraph{A page representation is a choice, not a given.} An \emph{observation
mode} is a complete, executable specification of what the agent is shown at a
step: which textual encoding of the page, framed by which prompt, accompanied by
which image, if any. Two modes can expose the same page and still lead the same
model to different actions. Once representation is a choice, it has a cost
curve, and once it has a cost curve it can be optimised.

\paragraph{Selection has an upper bound that nobody can reach.} If we look at
outcomes \emph{after} the fact and pick, per task, whichever mode happened to
succeed, we obtain a number that no deployable system can achieve. It is still
worth computing, because it bounds the entire enterprise: it is the most that
perfect selection could ever be worth. We call this the \emph{oracle ceiling},
and we are careful throughout to treat it as a bound on opportunity rather than
as a result.

\paragraph{An upper bound is not a plan.} Between the ceiling and a deployable
system sit two gaps that are usually collapsed in discussion and are kept apart
here. The first is \emph{predictability}: can the right mode be inferred from
signals available before the expensive mode is paid for? The second is
\emph{economics}: even a good predictor loses if escalation is rare, mistakes
are asymmetric, or the router costs more than it saves. The core methodological
commitment of this dissertation is that these three quantities --- ceiling,
predictability, realised value --- are measured separately and never allowed to
stand in for one another.

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

We construct a controlled representation space over a fixed agent and measure
along the chain just described.

Six observation modes are defined by crossing three binary factors --- the text
payload (accessibility-tree text vs.\ flattened \sommarks{} text), the prompt
framing (structured-text style vs.\ Set-of-Marks style), and the presence of the
annotated image. Four of the six send no image at all, one sends text and image
together, and one sends only the image. Everything else in the stack --- the
task, the model, the decoding parameters, the action space, the step budget, the
benchmark evaluator, the cost-accounting boundary --- is held identical across
modes. Chapter~\ref{ch:system} makes that claim auditable.

The sweep covers 8 (site, model) cells: three backbones spanning two model
families on two VisualWebArena sites \citep{koh2024visualwebarena}, plus two
backbones on a WebArena site \citep{zhou2024webarena} used as an external
validation setting. Every cell runs all six modes over the same fixed task set,
so all comparisons are paired at the task level.

On that base we then compute, in order: the retrospective oracle ceiling and
each mode's irreplaceable contribution to it; a measured noise floor, obtained
by rerunning \emph{unchanged} conditions, against which every effect size is
judged; a leakage-controlled predictive study of whether the routing target is
learnable from serving-time features; and an end-to-end comparison of the
resulting policy against fixed alternatives with cost counted.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{A controlled factorisation of the page-input space, and the
      measurement that it is dominated by one factor.} Six executable modes
      separate text payload, prompt framing and image presence over an otherwise
      fixed agent. The factorisation shows that the cost difference between the
      cheap and expensive conditions is almost entirely attributable to the
      image, which is what makes the image-off region worth studying at all
      (Chapter~\ref{ch:system}).

\item \textbf{A ceiling measurement reported next to its own repetition
      baseline.} A retrospective oracle gains $+3.45$ to $+16.35\pp$ of task
      success over the best single mode at 13.7--35.3\% lower cost, consistently
      across all 8 cells and both benchmarks. We pair this with a measured
      rerun floor from replicated identical conditions, and then push it to the
      matched arm count: one \emph{rerun} arm buys 2.0--7.6\pp{} by itself, and
      six reruns of the strongest single mode close to within 3.53\pp{} of the
      six-mode oracle --- below the threshold we ourselves derive. Applying that
      standard to our own headline moves the surviving claim from the ceiling's
      height to its price. To our knowledge, no comparable web-agent
      representation study reports its effect sizes against a measured
      same-condition floor, let alone an arm-count-matched repetition bound
      (Chapter~\ref{ch:repr}).

\item \textbf{A negative predictability result with the estimator explicitly
      exonerated.} Under a fully nested cross-validation protocol --- in which
      mode selection, threshold selection and feature standardisation all occur
      inside the training folds --- the learned triage router Pareto-beats the
      trivial always-cheapest fixed policy in \textbf{0 of 8} cells. Two
      independent controls (a bundle-permutation null at $B=10{,}000$, and the
      always-cheapest comparator itself) rule out the two cheapest alternative
      explanations (Chapters~\ref{ch:pred}--\ref{ch:e2e}).

\item \textbf{A mechanism for the failure, located in label supply rather than
      model capacity.} Routing labels exist only where some mode succeeded;
      at 2--27\% base success, 4 of 6 cells contain no trainable which-mode
      classifier at all, and three independent attempts to manufacture more
      supervision each fail for a structurally different reason. We additionally
      show that the strongest single predictor on VisualWebArena is the
      benchmark's own human difficulty annotation --- a feature no deployment
      possesses (Chapter~\ref{ch:pred}, Chapter~\ref{ch:disc}).

\item \textbf{A calibrated statement of what the negative result is a negative
      result \emph{about}.} We show that even the retrospective oracle fails to
      enter the winning region against always-cheapest in 7 of 8 cells, because
      always-cheapest is a cost lower bound. The correct conclusion is therefore
      that no profitable operating point exists under this cost boundary, not
      that the learner was inadequate (Chapter~\ref{ch:e2e}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{This dissertation contains a ``no'', and that is deliberate}
\label{sec:the-no}

The second half of the research question --- can the need be predicted cheaply
enough to be worth predicting? --- is answered in the negative. Two consequences
follow, and both are structural rather than cosmetic.

First, Chapter~\ref{ch:e2e} is not organised around how much the router won. It
is organised around \emph{where the chain breaks and why that break is a
property of the regime rather than of our implementation}. Second, the
scientific contribution lives in Chapter~\ref{ch:disc}: what this work delivers
is not a router but a boundary --- a characterisation of the conditions under
which representation routing cannot be learned.

A negative result can carry a dissertation only if two things are established
first, and we take both as obligations rather than as options. The opportunity
must be shown to exist, or ``we could not learn it'' collapses into ``there was
nothing there''; this is what the ceiling measurement in Chapter~\ref{ch:repr}
is for. And the failure must be traced to a named mechanism, or ``we could not
learn it'' collapses into ``your method was bad''; this is what the label-supply
diagnosis in Chapter~\ref{ch:pred} is for. Where our evidence stops short of
either obligation, we say so in place rather than in a closing list of caveats.

\section{What this dissertation does not claim}
\label{sec:not-claimed}

Four statements are consistent with our data but stronger than it, and we
therefore do not make them anywhere in this document.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item That the image-off modes \emph{replace} the full Set-of-Marks mode. Their
      interest is complementarity, and Chapter~\ref{ch:repr} shows even that is
      weaker than an earlier reading of our own data suggested.
\item That dropping the image costs little performance, or that the image-off
      configuration constitutes a cheaper Set-of-Marks. On four of six
      VisualWebArena cells the full mode is the strongest single mode.
\item That representation routing is unlearnable in general. We characterise why
      it is unlearnable \emph{in the observed 2--27\% base-success regime}, and
      Section~\ref{sec:regime-boundary} states what would have to change for the
      conclusion to change.
\item That reduced token or dollar cost constitutes a reduced environmental
      footprint. Section~\ref{sec:sustainability} names the exact measurement
      boundary and what it excludes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Map of the dissertation}
\label{sec:map}

Each chapter answers one question and hands the next chapter the question it
could not answer.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:bg}] asks what prior work already settles, and shows that
  four active research programmes each optimise a different decision variable
  than the one studied here.
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:system}] asks how representation choice can be compared
  fairly enough for later differences to be interpretable, and fixes the
  comparison boundary that the rest of the document depends on.
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:repr}] asks whether the modes differ enough for selection
  to be worth anything, establishes the ceiling, and then measures how much of
  it survives contact with a rerun.
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:pred}] asks whether the need can be predicted from
  serving-time signals, and separates ``predictable'' from ``useful''.
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:e2e}] asks whether acting on those predictions beats a
  fixed policy once cost is counted, and finds that the comparator itself
  explains most of the answer.
\item[Chapter~\ref{ch:disc}] reconciles the three results, states the regime in
  which they hold, and closes on what the work enables.
\end{description}

\chapterhandoff{This chapter has argued that per-step representation selection
is an efficiency problem with a measurable structure, and has set out the three
conditions under which it would be worth solving. It has not shown that the

literature on web-agent observations, model routing, selective prediction and
