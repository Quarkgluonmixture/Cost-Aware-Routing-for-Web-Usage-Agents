\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

A web agent must be given some encoding of the current page before it can act.
That encoding can be cheap structured text, an expensive annotated screenshot,
or both together, and the choice is normally made once and applied everywhere.
Since a task runs for tens of steps, it is also paid for tens of times. This
dissertation asks whether the expensive choice is necessary at every step, and
whether the steps where it \emph{is} necessary can be identified cheaply enough
for the identification to be worth making.

We build a controlled representation space over a fixed agent. Six observation
modes vary the page text payload, the prompt framing, and whether an annotated
image is sent; everything else stays put, including the task, the action space,
the step budget and the accounting boundary. The sweep covers 8 (site, model)
cells on two benchmarks, VisualWebArena and WebArena, across three backbones. On
it we measure three quantities in sequence: what a retrospective oracle would
gain by choosing per task, whether that choice is predictable from serving-time
features under a fully nested cross-validation protocol, and whether acting on
the predictions beats a trivial fixed policy once cost is counted.

The answers are, in order, \emph{yes}, \emph{no}, and \emph{the question is
mis-posed}. A retrospective oracle over the six modes gains $+3.45$ to $+16.35$
percentage points of task success over the best single mode while spending
1.6--35.3\% less, in every cell and on both benchmarks. Applying our own noise
standard to that headline deflates it. Adding one \emph{rerun} arm of an
unchanged mode buys 2.0--7.6 points by itself, and at matched arm count six
reruns of the strongest single mode come within 3.53 points of the six-mode
oracle, which is below the threshold we derive for what a single repetition can
produce on its own. What survives is the ceiling's price rather than its height,
since selection reaches that level for one episode per task where repetition
needs six. Under the nested protocol, the learned triage router Pareto-beats the
trivial always-cheapest fixed policy in \textbf{0 of 8} cells. So does the
retrospective oracle, in 7 of 8, which recasts the negative result from ``the
learner failed'' to ``no profitable operating point exists under this cost
boundary''.

We locate the obstruction in label supply rather than model capacity. Routing
labels are minted only when a task is solved, so at 2--27\% base success 4 of 6
cells fail the admissibility rule for a which-mode classifier, and the single
feature carrying most of the apparent discriminative power on VisualWebArena
turns out to be the benchmark's own human difficulty annotation, which no
deployment has. A direct undersampling control confirms the competing
explanation and then bounds it: learning curves are still rising, so more data
would buy a better predictor, but the limit of a better predictor is the oracle,
and the oracle wins in 1 of 8 cells. Pricing the constraint puts the benchmark
that would relieve it at 2.1 to 4.2 times the size of the ones that exist, which
is a specification rather than an impossibility.

The contribution is not a router. It is a measured boundary: the conditions
under which representation routing cannot be learned, with the opportunity
proven to exist before it is declared unreachable, and the failure traced to a
mechanism we can price.

% ---------- public access ----------------------------------------------------
\chapter*{Data, Code and Public Access}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Data, Code and Public Access}
\markboth{Data, Code and Public Access}{Data, Code and Public Access}

Every component of this study is publicly obtainable with one stated exception.
The benchmarks (VisualWebArena, WebArena) are open-source and self-hosted from
their published Docker images; two of the three backbones
(\texttt{Qwen3-VL-4B}, \texttt{google/gemma-3-4b-it}) are openly available model
weights run locally; the experiment harness, analysis pipeline and figure
scripts are released with the submission.

\textbf{The exception is the large backbone.} \texttt{Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B} was
served through a metered API proxy rather than self-hosted. Its outputs,
per-step logs and billing records are archived and released, but a third party
cannot re-run those conditions without equivalent proxy access. Results that
depend on that backbone alone are therefore \emph{replicable from our logs} but
not \emph{independently reproducible end to end}. Every claim that rests on
that backbone is marked where it is made, and
Chapters~\ref{ch:repr}--\ref{ch:e2e} are built so that none of the conclusions
depends on a single backbone.
